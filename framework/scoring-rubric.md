# The Idea Scorecard

Every idea is graded on **six lenses**, each scored **1–5**. Think of it like a
credit rating for a business idea: six separate checks, rolled into one number
out of 100.

| # | Lens | Default weight | The question it answers |
|---|------|---------------:|-------------------------|
| 1 | Market size | 20% | Is the prize big enough, and is it growing? |
| 2 | Founder fit | 20% | Am I unusually well placed to win this? |
| 3 | Competition | 15% | Is there room for a new player? |
| 4 | Funding landscape | 15% | Will money be available, or can it bootstrap? |
| 5 | Copycat moat | 15% | Once it works, can someone just clone it? |
| 6 | AI future-proofing | 15% | Does AI make this stronger, or make it obsolete? |

**Overall score** = Σ (lens score ÷ 5 × weight). Max 100.

| Score | Verdict | Meaning |
|------:|---------|---------|
| 75–100 | **Pursue** | Worth real time: customer interviews, a pilot, a deck. |
| 60–74 | **Explore** | Promising but has a weak spot. Name it and test it cheaply. |
| < 60 | **Pass** | Park it. Revisit only if something changes. |

**Kill flag:** any single lens scoring **1** gets flagged red no matter the total.
A great market doesn't rescue an idea a well-funded incumbent will crush tomorrow.

Weights can be changed per person in the dashboard, or permanently in
`profile/my-profile.md` under "My weights".

---

## 1. Market size

Measure **TAM** (everyone who could buy), **SAM** (the slice you can actually
reach), and **SOM** (what you could realistically win in 3–5 years). Always
build a bottom-up estimate (number of buyers × price × frequency) and sanity
check it against any top-down analyst figure.

| Score | Looks like |
|------:|-----------|
| 5 | SAM > $1B and growing > 15%/yr; a clear "why now" |
| 4 | SAM $250M–$1B, healthy growth |
| 3 | SAM $50–250M, or big but flat |
| 2 | SAM $10–50M; a lifestyle business at best |
| 1 | Under $10M, shrinking, or no evidence anyone pays for this today |

## 2. Founder fit

Scored against `profile/my-profile.md`. The question is about unfair
advantage, not mere competence.

| Score | Looks like |
|------:|-----------|
| 5 | Deep domain experience + network that contains the first 10 customers + a skill competitors lack |
| 4 | Strong in 2 of those 3 |
| 3 | Relevant transferable skills; would need a co-founder for a key gap |
| 2 | Interest but little edge; would be learning the industry from scratch |
| 1 | Works against strengths, or needs skills/credentials you don't have and can't hire early |

## 3. Competition

Map direct competitors, indirect substitutes (including "do nothing" and
"spreadsheet + an intern"), and incumbents who could add this as a feature.

| Score | Looks like |
|------:|-----------|
| 5 | Real demand, fragmented or poorly served; no dominant player |
| 4 | A few players with obvious, documented gaps (complaints in reviews, forums) |
| 3 | Crowded but differentiated positioning is possible |
| 2 | Two or three strong, well-funded leaders; you'd compete on price |
| 1 | A giant gives this away free or is clearly about to |

## 4. Funding landscape

Look at recent rounds in the space (last 24 months), who's investing, typical
round sizes, and exits. Also judge capital need: can it reach revenue cheaply?

| Score | Looks like |
|------:|-----------|
| 5 | Active investor appetite AND can reach revenue cheaply (optionality) |
| 4 | Either strong appetite or easily bootstrappable |
| 3 | Mixed signals; fundable with strong traction |
| 2 | Investors are cooling or the space has had visible failures; capital-heavy |
| 1 | Needs a lot of capital and investors are avoiding the category |

## 5. Copycat moat

The question: if this works and gets attention, what stops a fast follower?
Check for network effects, proprietary data, switching costs, regulatory
licences, brand/trust, exclusive distribution, and community.

| Score | Looks like |
|------:|-----------|
| 5 | Two or more moats that compound with time (e.g. data + network effects) |
| 4 | One strong moat that deepens with each customer |
| 3 | Moat comes from relationships/brand/execution speed only |
| 2 | Easy to copy once seen; advantage lasts months |
| 1 | A weekend project for a competent team |

## 6. AI future-proofing

Ask three questions:
1. **Replace:** could a general AI assistant do the core job for the customer in 2–3 years?
2. **Commoditize:** will AI make the product so cheap to build that margins collapse?
3. **Amplify:** does AI make *your* version cheaper, better, or more defensible than before?

| Score | Looks like |
|------:|-----------|
| 5 | AI is a tailwind; the value sits in trust, accountability, physical world, regulation, proprietary data or relationships |
| 4 | AI amplifies it; core value is hard for a general model to replicate |
| 3 | Neutral; some tasks automate, the core holds |
| 2 | A thin layer on top of a model; platforms likely to absorb it |
| 1 | The product *is* something a general AI assistant will do for free |

## Evidence standard

- Every score needs at least one cited source or an explicit reasoning chain.
- Mark each number as **Sourced** (link), **Estimated** (show the math) or **Assumption** (say so).
- Give a confidence level per lens: High / Medium / Low.
- If a fact is older than 18 months, say so.
